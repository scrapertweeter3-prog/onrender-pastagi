Show the serving math or it did not happen

Most AI coverage repeats a vendor claim at full width. PastAGI starts from the numbers a claim would need to survive an audit. A text-to-speech model gets reviewed with its serving math attached. An hourly price for a frontier model gets checked line by line rather than quoted. The site's standing position is that chain-of-thought summaries are not transparency, and its articles on reasoning visibility argue exactly where the trace ends and the summary begins.

What gets covered

Practical build topics carry the editorial weight: inference chips and the GPU market they are reshaping, agents that leak sensitive data and the patterns that stop it, prompt-dependency graphs that shrink a retest set when a prompt changes. Each piece belongs to a section a working builder can navigate: guides, tools, engineering, use cases, and an archive that keeps the older audits reachable.

Benchmarks get treated as claims

A benchmark number on pastagi.com is an input to a check, not a conclusion. The reviews ask what the metric measures, what the serving conditions were, and whether a paraphrase-drift or false-positive-rate view would change the ranking. When the math is missing, the article says so instead of rounding up to enthusiasm.
